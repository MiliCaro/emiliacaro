Emilia Caro

Your postcode matters more than your genetic code.

In Argentina, depending on the province where they are born, a baby is almost three times more likely to die before their first birthday. And a woman, almost four times more likely to die of cervical cancer, one of the most preventable deaths there is. Biology does not explain that distance: access, prevention and the way we organize health systems do.

Perspective

Inequality is not a social issue that affects health: it is a cause of death, measurable and avoidable.

Cervical cancer

The gap between Argentina's provinces is wider than the gap between the country and the most developed nations. Some provinces have figures like the least developed countries; others come close to the most developed. That is not biology: it is organization.

Prevention is the cheapest equity policy there is.

Bio

A career across science, management and policy

Emilia Caro works at the intersection of science, management and public policy in health. Trained in molecular biology and human genetics, with an MBA in health management, she has spent over two decades in the governance and transformation of health systems.

She serves as Global Chair of Women in Global Health, President of its Argentine chapter, and Executive Director of Fundación GEDYT, and sits on the Board of GEDYT. Her work spans health equity, the design of care models, and the strengthening of institutions that decide how health is governed.
